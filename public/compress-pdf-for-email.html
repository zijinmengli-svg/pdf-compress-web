Compress a PDF for Email

Make a report, presentation, invoice bundle, or portfolio easier to send when an attachment is rejected or slow to upload.

Practical starting point: 20MB leaves some room under common attachment limits. Use a lower number when the message includes other files or the recipient has a stricter mailbox.

Email size caveat: attachments grow during email encoding, so a PDF that sits exactly at the provider limit can still fail. Leave a margin instead of targeting the final byte.

Temporary processing

Files are automatically removed after about one hour, so download the result before composing the message.

Why an attachment can exceed the stated limit

Email systems encode binary files for transport. That adds overhead before the message reaches the recipient. Signatures, inline images, and additional attachments also count toward the total, while corporate mail gateways may enforce a lower limit than the sender's service.

  • For a 25MB mailbox limit, a target around 18-20MB is more reliable than 25MB.
  • If several people need the document, a cloud link may avoid duplicate mailbox storage.
  • For confidential material, confirm the recipient's approved delivery method before choosing email.

Check before sending

  1. Open the compressed result and verify the page count.
  2. Zoom into charts, signatures, and small invoice details.
  3. Use a clear filename so the recipient can identify the document.
  4. Attach the compressed copy, not the original file with a similar name.
