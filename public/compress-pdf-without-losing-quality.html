Compress a PDF Without Losing Visible Quality

Choose the largest acceptable target and evaluate the downloaded result instead of relying on a generic high, medium, or low setting.

Practical starting point: enter 20MB, or the largest size your destination accepts. A higher target gives the compressor more room to preserve resolution and image detail.

Honest limitation: substantial file-size reduction cannot always be lossless. PDFs dominated by photographs or scans must discard image information to become much smaller.

Quality-first behavior

If the requested target cannot be reached at usable settings, TinyPDF may return the smallest usable result instead of destroying readability.

What can stay unchanged

Page count, dimensions, layout, and vector text often survive compression well. The largest savings usually come from images, duplicated resources, and inefficient source exports. A text-heavy report can shrink dramatically with little visible difference; a photo catalogue cannot.

  • Usually resilient: selectable text, simple vectors, page geometry, and basic forms.
  • Needs inspection: photographs, scans, soft shadows, transparent effects, and very small diagrams.
  • Risky at extreme targets: technical drawings, fine art reproductions, and documents already compressed several times.

Use a repeatable quality check

  1. Select one page with small text, one with a photograph, and one with linework.
  2. Compare those pages at the zoom level your reader will actually use.
  3. If quality is insufficient, raise the target or return to the original source file.
  4. Avoid compressing an already compressed result; always restart from the best original.
