The tightest limit, handled honestly

Set the target below to 1MB and TinyPDF will search for the smallest version of your document that is still worth reading.

Please read this first: 1MB is smaller than a single high-resolution scan. Short text-heavy documents usually survive it well. Picture-heavy files will come out noticeably degraded, and some cannot reach 1MB at all. If your document is image-based, try 2MB or 5MB instead.

What survives at 1MB, and what does not

This target is worth understanding before you spend time on it. Here is the honest split:

  • Usually fine: contracts, forms, statements, short reports, and any document that is mostly real text.
  • Usually poor: scanned photo albums, image-heavy brochures, anything with full-colour photography.
  • Often impossible: long documents where pages have already been scanned and compressed once.

The reason is simple. A page of genuine text costs very little to store, while a picture of that same page costs hundreds of times more. Compression cannot change what kind of data the file contains.

Who asks for something this small

  1. Fax gateways. Internet fax services apply bandwidth-era limits to everything passing through them.
  2. Legacy enterprise systems. Internal tools built years ago still carry their original upload caps.
  3. Low-bandwidth regions. Sending a heavy attachment over a slow connection fails more often than people expect.
  4. Email footer or preview attachments. Anything that has to load instantly inside another page.

Getting the best result out of 1MB

  1. OCR before compressing. Turning pictures of text into text is the single biggest saving available.
  2. Use grayscale. Unless a signature or stamp needs colour, black and white is enough.
  3. Scan at about 150 DPI. Higher resolution buys nothing at this file size.
  4. Remove non-essential pages. A cover sheet can alone be worth half a megabyte.

Compress PDF to 1MB: common questions

Can TinyPDF always reach 1MB?

No. Many files contain more image data than fits in 1MB at any usable quality. When the target cannot be reached, TinyPDF returns the smallest usable result instead of pushing further and leaving you with unreadable pages.

Will the text still be selectable after such heavy compression?

Text stays selectable in most cases, but reaching a target this small sometimes requires rasterizing pages. Rasterized pages are images, so their text is no longer selectable or searchable. The status message tells you when rasterization was used.

What is the best way to prepare a file for 1MB?

Convert scans to real text with OCR first, since genuine text is far cheaper to store than a picture of text. Then use grayscale, scan around 150 DPI, and remove any page that carries no information.

How long do you keep my files, and do I need an account?

No account and no payment are required. Uploaded and compressed files are temporary and are automatically deleted after about one hour, so download your result soon after compression finishes.
