Who usually needs a 20MB ceiling

Unlike a "small / medium / high" quality slider, a target size lets you aim at the exact number the form actually enforces. 20MB shows up most often in these places:

  • Thesis and dissertation repositories. Chapters with maps, plates, or microscopy images push files past the cap even when the text is light.
  • Tender and procurement uploads. Bid packages bundle scanned certificates, and portals usually reject rather than warn.
  • Journal supplementary material. Reviewers need the file to open cleanly, so layout changes are risky.
  • Decks with embedded media. Pitch decks exported with linked video or full-resolution screenshots are the usual offenders.

Upload limits change without notice, so always confirm the number on your recipient's own help page before you submit.

What to do if 20MB still looks too big

  1. Re-export at print quality, not best quality. Most desktop publishing tools ship a "best quality" preset that nobody can see the difference from.
  2. Flatten transparency and layers. Layered artwork forces the PDF to carry several versions of the same image.
  3. Replace full-page scans of plain text with real text. An OCR pass often shrinks a scanned chapter by more than half.
  4. Try 10MB instead. If the form allows it, you will usually still get a perfectly readable document.

Compress PDF to 20MB: common questions

Can TinyPDF guarantee my file lands exactly on 20MB?

It aims to land at or just under 20MB. TinyPDF searches for the clearest version of your document that still fits the target. Some PDFs have limited room for compression, and in those cases it returns the smallest usable result rather than damaging the file.

Will compressing to 20MB hurt the quality of my figures?

Usually not. 20MB is a generous target, so most theses, reports, and decks survive it with little visible change. You will notice the difference mainly on full-page scans and uncompressed photos, so check those pages before submitting.

Do the page count and page size stay the same?

Yes. TinyPDF is designed to keep the same page count, page dimensions, and layout. Only the internal image and stream data are recompressed.

How long do you keep my files, and do I need an account?

No account and no payment are required. Uploaded and compressed files are temporary and are automatically deleted after about one hour, so download your result soon after compression finishes.
