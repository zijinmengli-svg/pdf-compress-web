Compress a PDF Exported from Figma

Shrink a case study, prototype walkthrough, or design handoff after Figma creates a PDF larger than the destination accepts.

Practical starting point: use 10MB for a short visual case study. A longer deck with many screenshots may need 20MB or a smaller selection of frames.

Figma caveat: blurred shadows, image fills, and large device screenshots consume far more space than vectors. Tiny labels can soften when the target is pushed too low.

Prepare frames before exporting

Figma exports every selected top-level frame as a PDF page. A hidden alternative, duplicate iteration, or oversized mood board can add megabytes without helping the reader. Export only the frames that belong in the final narrative.

  • Flatten decorative image effects when editability is not required.
  • Crop image fills to the visible area instead of keeping large unused originals.
  • Avoid placing a full-resolution screenshot inside a small device frame.

Inspect the result like a reviewer

Open the downloaded PDF at normal reading size, then zoom into one dense screen. Check 12px labels, thin dividers, charts, and contrast on tinted backgrounds. If these fail, raise the target or return to Figma and simplify the heaviest pages.
